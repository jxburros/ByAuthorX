# Author workflow evaluation

Test the product direction with five people who write source-based reports:
researchers, analysts, technical writers or consultants. Use documents and sources
they are authorized to share. This is a manual study plan, not an active recruitment
or telemetry system; ByAuthorX does not upload usage measurements.

Give each participant one task: attach their research, improve a short passage,
inspect its evidence, accept a useful suggestion and export the deliverable. Let
them discover the introduction and guide before giving help. Offer a configured
local model or explicitly authorized remote provider if they choose AI review.

Record, with consent:

| Observation | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Time to first useful accepted suggestion | Minutes from starting the task; record no successful suggestion separately |
| Evidence comprehension | Can they distinguish quotation matching from support for a claim? |
| Author control | Can they preserve numbers/quotes/terms and accept only one useful change? |
| Publication confidence | Can they identify excluded private material and locate format losses? |
| Export quality | Corrections needed when opening the output in their normal application |
| Return use | At an agreed follow-up, did they choose ByAuthorX for another real document, and why? |

Record where help was needed, rejected suggestions and reasons, unexpected losses,
and whether the workflow was easier than their current process. Do not infer
demand from feature counts or a small study's percentages. Use repeated obstacles
to choose the next improvement; retain failures as evidence.

Current implementation: research guide/sample, local evidence inspection,
preservation controls, readable proposal review and publication preview. It does
not prove product-market fit. Physical acceptance, export fidelity and long-document
responsiveness should be assessed before asking participants to trust their only
copy of a manuscript to the app.
