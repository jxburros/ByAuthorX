# Competitors cite passages, but nobody proves support

The wider October 2026 field confirms the earlier report's main bet and sharpens it. Across about 35 more products (office suites, general AI assistants, academic tools, PKM and reading apps, fiction and long-form tools, and local-first RAG apps), **none verifies that a cited passage supports the sentence it is attached to and publishes accuracy for that check**. The best of them now attach passage-level citations that open the source. That set includes Gemini Notebook (the renamed NotebookLM), ChatGPT company knowledge, Readwise's Global Ghostreader, Zotero's Beaver plugin, Elicit and Ai2 Asta. Independent studies show why this is not enough. Grounded academic tools have mostly stopped inventing references, but they still misattribute, drift between identical runs, and falsely report that relevant material is absent. On the web, AI search tools got news citations wrong in **more than 60%** of Tow Center tests, and **45%** of EBU/BBC answers had a significant issue. Paperpal is the only vendor that markets a "citation relevance" check on the user's own manuscript, and it publishes no accuracy data. Three things change the roadmap. First, local RAG apps show that the most common local failure is the model's context window silently truncating retrieved text, not retrieval itself. Second, citation-link fidelity bugs recur across products (wrong page, wrong spot, leaks from unselected collections), so they belong in the evaluation harness. Third, MCP access to a personal library is now table stakes rather than a leapfrog. A large writer segment also now treats "no generative AI" as a feature. ByAuthorX should therefore lead with verified retrieval, continuity checking and auditing of the user's own citations, ahead of generation.

## Every surveyed product now pins sources; ByAuthorX's edge is verification and transparency

The newly researched products fall into a few recognisable designs. **Curated containers** make the user choose the context boundary: Gemini Notebook, Claude and ChatGPT Projects, Perplexity Spaces, Lex knowledge bases, AnythingLLM workspaces and Msty Knowledge Stacks. **Automatic permission-trimmed indexes** retrieve across everything a user can access: the Microsoft Graph semantic index, ChatGPT company knowledge, and Craft's and (historically) Coda's assistants. **Deterministic story-knowledge tools** inject entity entries by name: Sudowrite and Novelcrafter. A large **no-generative-AI camp** competes on structure alone: Scrivener, Plottr, Dabble, World Anvil, and Ulysses apart from Apple's OS-level tools. Local RAG apps follow one recipe almost uniformly. Documents that fit go in whole, as a "pinned" or "full context" item. Everything else is chunked into roughly 1,000-character to 1,000-token pieces with 10–20% overlap, embedded, and returned as the top 3–25 chunks above a similarity floor, with reranking as an optional "accuracy" mode.

The earlier report treated the Gemini Notebook rename as secondhand; it is now confirmed by Google. On **16 July 2026** Google announced that "NotebookLM is now Gemini Notebook", citing **30M+ users and 600,000+ organizations**, and added a per-notebook "secure cloud computer" that runs code over the sources ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/)). Official limits are **500,000 words or 200 MB per source** and **50 to 600 sources per notebook** depending on plan ([NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187); [plan table](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16213268?hl=en)). The earlier table's "Graph (details not researched)" cell can also now be filled from Microsoft's own documentation. The semantic index is "an advanced lexical and semantic index" that combines vectors, keyword matching and "personalization and social matching". Copilot calls it twice: once to ground the prompt and again in a **post-processing step after the LLM responds**. Users can scope retrieval to a SharePoint library or folder, and the library's column metadata then becomes a ranking signal. Indexing "can't be disabled" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftsearch/semantic-index-for-copilot)).

The general assistants have converged on automatic retrieval once projects outgrow the context window. Claude switches Projects to RAG automatically and shows a "RAG-enabled" indicator, but users cannot control when it happens ([Anthropic Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11473015-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-for-projects)). Anthropic's claim that quality stays "consistent" is vendor-reported. The early-trigger bug report cited earlier could not be reconfirmed. ChatGPT company knowledge lists the specific snippets it used, and each citation opens the original. That description comes from press coverage, not OpenAI documentation ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openai-launches-company-knowledge-in-chatgpt-letting-you-access-your-firms)). Apple's Writing Tools remain selection-only and ungrounded. Apple's models now rest on a Gemini base ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/22/google-gemini-powered-siri-2026)), and Ulysses v41 (September 2026) simply exposes Apple's Writing Tools in its toolbar ([Ulysses release notes](https://ulysses.app/release-notes/)).

## Grounded tools stopped inventing papers but still misattribute them

The independent evidence splits cleanly by architecture. On open-web news, the **Tow Center/CJR** study ran 1,600 queries across eight AI search tools, each asking them to identify the source article of a quoted passage. The tools were wrong **more than 60%** of the time. Perplexity did best at **37%** failure and Grok-3 worst at **94%**. Gemini and Grok-3 produced more fabricated links than correct ones, the tools rarely declined to answer, and premium tiers were **more confidently wrong** than free ones ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/03/ai-search-engines-fail-to-produce-accurate-citations-in-over-60-of-tests-according-to-new-tow-center-study/)). The **EBU/BBC** study covered 22 public broadcasters, 14 languages and over 3,000 responses. It found **45%** of answers had at least one significant issue ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/npr-extra/2025/10/21/g-s1-94424/global-study-on-news-integrity-in-ai-assistants-shows-need-for-safeguards-and-improved-accuracy)). Sourcing was the largest category at **31%**, and Gemini was worst at about **76%**. Those breakdowns come from secondary reports ([RadioInfo](https://radioinfo.com.au/?p=235432)). Heise reports a drop from 51% to 37% in "problematic" answers, which conflicts with the 45% headline. The difference is probably a narrower metric, but that is unconfirmed ([Heise](https://heise.de/-10798062)). In both studies, attribution failed more often than the answer text itself.

Academic tools grounded in a scholarly index do far better at existence and worse at relevance. In a peer-reviewed Reference Hallucination Score test, **Elicit and SciSpace scored 1** (negligible), Perplexity 7, and ChatGPT-3.5 and Bing **11** (critical). Keyword relevance was the most-hallucinated element at **61.6%** ([JMIR Med Inform, PMC11325115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11325115/)). A study of Scopus AI, Elicit, SciSpace and Scite found **no fabricated references**, only inaccurate or missing bibliographic details ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383988574_Correctness_and_Quality_of_References_generated_by_AI-based_Research_Assistant_Tools_The_Case_of_Scopus_AI_Elicit_SciSpace_and_Scite_in_the_Field_of_Business_Administration)). Ai2's OpenScholar paper reports GPT-4o hallucinating **over 90%** of cited papers on biomedical questions, against near zero for its retrieval-grounded 8B model. That is an author-reported result ([arXiv 2411.14199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14199)).

The residual failures are the ones ByAuthorX's design targets. An independent June 2026 audit of Asta's "Summarise Literature" found **unstable cited references across identical queries** and **little overlap between retrieved and cited documents**, which points to opaque selection. It used only 10 queries ([arXiv 2606.08301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08301)). For user-supplied documents, NotebookLM hallucinated **13%** of the time against **40%** for ChatGPT and Gemini in one academic test ([arXiv 2509.25498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25498)). A literary-analysis study found it **wrongly asserting that examples were absent** when they were present, and eventually fabricating examples under repeated questioning ([arXiv 2412.18270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18270)). A 2026 blog post describes the same "source blindness" after the Gemini 3.1 Pro transition, but its sourcing is unclear ([ud.hk](https://ud.hk/en/blogs/insight/article/2026-07-07-notebooklm-no-hallucination)). For a novelist asking "did I ever mention the scar?", a false "no" is as damaging as an invented "yes". The earlier report's abstention design only guarded against the second error.

Recall evidence for discovery tools is thin and mostly vendor-run. Elicit's figures, relayed secondhand, are about **95%** search recall across 994 Cochrane reviews ([CASRAI relay](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/elicit-ai-research-assistant)). An SMU library test measured Undermind at **72.7% recall@10** against a systematic-review gold standard ([SMU Libraries](https://library.smu.edu.sg/topics-insights/google-scholar-vs-ai-academic-search-expanding-literature-review-toolkit)). Reviewing Consensus, Aaron Tay found its Meter to be "vote counting" that ignores sample size and study quality, and its rankings shifted between repeat runs ([Aaron Tay](https://aarontay.substack.com/p/a-2025-deep-dive-of-consensus-promises)). The broader contamination problem is growing: one 2026 audit, reported secondhand, estimated **146,932 hallucinated citations** in 2025 preprints, prompting arXiv to tighten its policy ([SMU Libraries](https://library.smu.edu.sg/topics-insights/arxiv-tightens-policy-hallucinated-references-what-researchers-should-know-about)). No independent study evaluates citation accuracy over *user-uploaded* files in Claude Projects, ChatGPT Projects or Gemini Gems, which is the case closest to ByAuthorX.

## Writers' unmet needs cluster around research search, continuity and trust

The fiction and long-form evidence reframes who ByAuthorX is competing with. Plottr announced experimental AI brainstorming in 2024 and then publicly reversed course. Its January 2026 statement says it is not building AI and wants to stay "human-crafted" ([Plottr in 2026](https://plottr.com/plottr-in-2026/); [Plottr's AI Future](https://plottr.com/plottrs-ai-future/)). Dabble promises it "will never put words on the page for you" ([Dabble](https://www.dabblewriter.com/docs/reference/dabble-is-ai-free)). World Anvil says AI features are "not within the scope" ([World Anvil](https://www.worldanvil.com/community/voting/suggestion/182ee13a-ceb8-4bb8-b53d-904bfb2a326e/view)). A Literature & Latte-affiliated poster rejected subscriptions, provider lock-in and copyright exposure as reasons not to add AI to Scrivener ([L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/adding-ai-tools-to-scrivener/133222)). Sudowrite, meanwhile, fights long-manuscript drift mostly with bigger models. It markets new models on "Story Bible adherence" and pitches large-context Gemini Flash for "full-manuscript" drafting, and its September 2026 chat update fixed "dropped-context issues" ([Sudowrite changelog](https://feedback.sudowrite.com/changelog)). Neither camp offers retrieval over research files with citations back to the source passage.

That leaves four unmet needs that ByAuthorX's architecture fits unusually well. The first is **searchable research**. Scrivener users cannot reliably search or highlight inside imported PDFs, especially on iOS. They fall back to Spotlight or DEVONthink, and they ask for research shared across projects ([L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/searching-for-text-in-research-pdf/46155); [L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/better-web-pages-in-research-shared-research/32994)). The second is **continuity across chapters and books**. Sudowrite reviews summarise the AI as forgetting established details, and series writers describe cross-book errors such as a character who loses two fingers in book one and uses both hands in book three ([Marlvel, secondary](https://marlvel.ai/apps/sudowrite-ai-novel-writing/reviews); [Nerdbot, secondary](https://nerdbot.com/?p=300317)). The third is **low-maintenance story knowledge**. Bibles go unused when they live apart from the draft ([Novela, vendor](https://novela.so/en/blog/story-bible-that-gets-used)). Novelcrafter's Chat-to-Codex extraction and created/updated dates point to proposal-based upkeep ([Novelcrafter changelog](https://www.novelcrafter.com/changelog/)). The fourth is **trust**: in the same Scrivener thread, writers cite invented books and ISBNs, distrust data handling and want AI optional ([L&L forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/adding-ai-tools-to-scrivener/133222)).

Academic and PKM users voice parallel needs. Beaver users ask for OCR, Ollama support, BibTeX citation keys and clear lists of skipped files, and they report failures on 22,000-PDF libraries ([Zotero Forums](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/126573/beaver-zotero-ai-plugin-to-chat-with-your-library-organize-discover-research-and-read-papers)). Zotero itself ships every **6–10 weeks**. Zotero 9 (April 2026) added **inserting annotations directly into word-processor documents**, and Zotero 10 (August 2026) added accent-insensitive full-text search and nested advanced search. Zotero still ships no library Q&A assistant ([Zotero blog](https://www.zotero.org/blog/); [PapersGPT, competitor](https://www.papersgpt.com/en/blogs/zotero-9-10-whats-new-ai)). That raises the floor for non-AI citation workflows that ByAuthorX's Zotero integration must match. Privacy claims draw scrutiny. Reflect's end-to-end encryption alongside cloud GPT-4 features prompted the argument that E2EE and cloud AI cannot apply to the same request ([calmara, competitor blog](https://calmara.app/blog/why-ai-note-apps-arent-end-to-end-encrypted); [Privacy Guides](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/reflect-app-encrypted-note-taking-app/27531.md)). ByAuthorX's per-request consent preview is the honest answer to that critique, and it should be marketed in Dabble-style plain language.

Local-first tools reveal a failure the earlier report missed. Open WebUI's own troubleshooting guide names **context truncation** as the top RAG problem. Ollama sets its default context from VRAM, at **4,096 tokens below 24 GiB**, which silently trims retrieved passages ([Open WebUI](https://docs.openwebui.com/troubleshooting/rag)). Citation-fidelity bugs recur across the category. GPT4All fixed references leaking from **unselected collections** ([GPT4All releases](https://github.com/nomic-ai/gpt4all/releases)), Open WebUI documents Docling citations pointing to the **wrong page** ([Open WebUI](https://docs.openwebui.com/troubleshooting/rag)), and Readwise fixed links that jumped to the **wrong spot** ([Readwise](https://readwise.io/reader/update-aug2026)). All three are attribution bugs that retrieval-quality metrics would not catch.

## Roadmap changes: twelve additions, four revisions, and the confirmations

The new evidence leaves the earlier sequence (harness first, then wiring fixes, then models) intact. It adds several low-cost items and changes the weight of others. The earlier quick wins Q2 (hybrid in AI paths), Q3 (cross-library fusion), Q5 (normalized quote verification) and Q8 (cache-ordered prompts) are **confirmed** without change. Every serious competitor now runs hybrid retrieval: Microsoft, Readwise, Consensus, GPT4All and Open WebUI. Two items need updating. Q1, the evaluation harness, should gain attribution tests. Q7, the citation chips, should match Beaver and Readwise's hover-then-highlight behaviour.

Two of these deserve emphasis. **V1** changes the ordering of the earlier medium-term item 7. The cascade was planned for AI-written claims, but the only competitor marketing claim-support checking (Paperpal) applies it to the writer's own references, and academic users will compare against that. The deterministic first stage of the cascade already covers most of what V1 needs. **Q14** fills a gap the earlier abstention design left open. CRAG-style escalation guards against weak evidence, but a confident false "not found" is the documented NotebookLM failure, and an exhaustive local lexical search makes absence claims checkable in a way cloud tools cannot cheaply offer.

One earlier recommendation needs a caveat rather than a change. The earlier report proposed merging chunks up to about 300–500 tokens. Local-tool guidance clusters higher, from AnythingLLM's 1,000 characters to Open WebUI's 1,000 tokens with 100 overlap for local models. Tutorials suggest 300–400 tokens when "AI ignores document content" ([insiderllm, secondary](https://insiderllm.com/guides/anythingllm-setup-guide/)). The sources disagree, and the evidence for any default is anecdotal, so chunk size should be a parameter swept by the Q1 harness rather than fixed. The earlier recommendation of Docling as a PDF quality path also stands, but Open WebUI's wrong-page bug makes Docling page mapping an explicit Q1+ test case.

## How much of this to trust, and what is still unknown

The evidence base is uneven, and the main weaknesses should be stated plainly. **Vendor-reported** and unverified capability claims include:

- Elicit's "sentence-level" citations and its 95% recall, 97–99% screening and 96% extraction figures, which are relayed secondhand.
- Paperpal's "citation relevance" check and its 250M+ database.
- Jenni's "Claim confidence" and its whole feature description, which comes from a vendor-authored comparison.
- Mem's "grounded" fact cards, as shipped in 2024.
- Anthropic's statement that RAG quality stays consistent in Projects.
- OpenScholar's results and Undermind's whitepaper.

**Secondary-sourced** claims include:

- ChatGPT company knowledge snippet citations and Projects file caps.
- Perplexity file limits, which conflict across sources.
- Grammarly's Citation Finder and AI Detector.
- The EBU/BBC per-assistant breakdown and Heise's conflicting 37% figure.
- Apple's iOS 27 Writing Tools changes, which are leak-based.
- Campfire update details and LivingWriter's features.
- The 146,932 hallucinated-citations estimate.

**Unverified or contradicted** items include:

- Coda Brain's current status: the product page now redirects to a help article that returned 403.
- Khoj Cloud's reported April 2026 shutdown, which one listing contradicts.
- World Anvil's "AI Sage", which appears only on a competitor's blog and is contradicted by World Anvil's own reply.
- Whether Plottr ever shipped and withdrew AI features.
- NotebookLM's passage-chip UI details, its September 2026 quota model, and the earlier Claude early-RAG-trigger report.

The research gaps that matter most for ByAuthorX are these:

- **No independent benchmark of citation accuracy over user-uploaded files** for any general assistant.
- **No independent accuracy figures for any claim-support checker** (Paperpal, Jenni, Elicit, Scite).
- No first-party documentation of citation granularity for Copilot in Word, Claude Projects, Mem 2026, Tana, Heptabase or Capacities chat.
- No Gemini Gem knowledge limits.
- No 2026 Scite status.
- No Craft 2026 changelog confirming that local models remain.
- No Reddit or r/LocalLLaMA complaint corpus for any category.
- No quantitative survey of how many writers reject AI.
- No user study of whether people actually open citation chips or context panels, which is the over-reliance risk behind every pattern in this report.
- Licenses for the local tools were confirmed only for Khoj (AGPL-3.0) and Reor (AGPL-3.0). Both are disqualifying as embedded components unless ByAuthorX's own license allows AGPL.

## Conclusion

The competitive frontier has shifted from "can the assistant cite?" to "can the citation be trusted?", and the evidence now points to three specific trust failures: misattribution, false absence and broken links between a citation and its source. Each has a cheap, local, deterministic first defence: span-level quote matching, exhaustive lexical absence checks, and fidelity tests in the harness. A desktop app can run all three on every request without consent prompts or cloud costs, which a cloud suite cannot match economically or on privacy. This is a structural advantage, not a feature race, and it also answers the many writers who reject generative AI. A tool that checks, finds and remembers, without writing anything, is acceptable to that camp in a way Sudowrite is not.

The strategic risk has also changed. Hybrid retrieval, pinned sources, passage chips and MCP access are now the minimum expected, so ByAuthorX gains little from reaching parity on them and loses badly if it lags. What remains unclaimed is a published, reproducible accuracy number for grounding over a user's own library. No vendor, academic tool or independent study provides one. If ByAuthorX publishes its own retrieval and citation-support benchmarks, including false-absence and link-fidelity rates, those numbers would differentiate it more than any single feature.
