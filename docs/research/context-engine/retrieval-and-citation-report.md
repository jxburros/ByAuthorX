# Wire verified retrieval into ByAuthorX's provenance core

ByAuthorX already has the hardest and least common part of a grounded writing assistant: content-hashed sources, locator-precise citation marks, verification statuses recomputed rather than trusted, passage-level consent previews and prompt-injection framing. What it lacks is the retrieval engine and the verification layer that should sit on top of that core. Its AI paths still send the user's raw prompt to a keyword-only, OR-joined SQLite FTS5 query, even when embeddings are configured. Citations are checked only for ID existence or exact substrings, and there is no evaluation harness. The state of the art as of October 2026 has four parts. Retrieval combines lexical and dense search, adds context-enriched chunks and a reranker; Anthropic reports this cuts top-20 retrieval failures by **67%**. A per-request router chooses among no retrieval, targeted lookup, agentic multi-hop search and cached full-document context. Citations come from native source-span APIs. Each claim is then checked by a cascade: deterministic quote matching first, then a local entailment model, then an optional LLM judge. No competitor examined ships that full combination. Microsoft Word's passage-level "deep citations" were still unshipped in early October 2026. Google's Gemini-in-Docs grounds on a curated notebook but can attach real passages that do not support the sentence. Fiction tools inject context by entity name with no semantic retrieval and no citations. ByAuthorX can therefore become the best grounded word processor by finishing work its own spec already calls for: wire hybrid search into the AI, add a local reranker and entailment check, ship a transparent router and context inspector, and measure all of it. It should not bolt on an opaque cloud RAG.

## No shipping word processor proves that a citation supports its sentence

The competitive bar in late 2026 has three table-stakes features: explicit source pinning, workspace-wide retrieval, and clickable document-level citations. Grounding quality, though, stops at "the passage exists". **Microsoft 365 Copilot in Word** can reference up to 20 files, emails or meetings plus folders. Those are 2025-era limits, reported secondhand and not reconfirmed for 2026 ([ElevenForum repost of Microsoft blog](https://elevenforum.com/t/expanding-reference-capabilities-with-microsoft-365-copilot-in-word.35466)). Folder references use the **10 most recent files**, a recency heuristic rather than a relevance one ([handsontek](https://m365admin.handsontek.net/?p=13476)). As of early October 2026, Word citations were opt-in plain text with links, not footnotes or APA/MLA. Auto-footnote citations promised for September had not appeared, and passage-linked "deep citations" (roadmap item 523223) had slipped to a **November 2026 GA** ([Office Watch](https://office-watch.com/2026/copilot-word-citations-image-editing/); [Microsoft roadmap](https://www.microsoft.com/releasecommunications/api/v2/m365/523223)). Office Watch also notes that a Word citation can point at a superseded draft, and that the cited source may not say what Copilot claims. Whether deep citations actually shipped is unconfirmed.

**Google** has gone furthest on curated grounding. Since 23 September 2026, typing "@" in Docs grounds Gemini on an existing Gemini Notebook (the renamed NotebookLM) with inline citations ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/ground-ai-prompts-in-google-docs-on-existing-sources-from-Gemini-Notebook.html)). Since May 2026, notebooks keep Drive sources in sync automatically ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/keep-your-sources-up-to-date-with-automatic-Drive-syncing-in-NotebookLM.html)). A practitioner guide still warns that Gemini "can cite a real passage that doesn't support the sentence it's attached to" ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/gemini-google-docs-integration.md)). The rename itself is secondhand ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/alifar/google-renames-notebooklm-to-gemini-notebook-as-workspace-integration-expands-5811)). **Notion** markets "verified content and citations, using only the sources you choose" ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/product/enterprise-search)), but nothing found shows per-claim entailment checking.

Long-form fiction tools solved a different problem: getting the right story facts into the prompt. **Novelcrafter's Codex** auto-detects entity names and aliases in the prompt and the preceding text. It adds those entries in a documented order (mentions, manual scene references, POV character, global entries, child entries) ([Novelcrafter help](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/faq/ai-and-prompting/codex-context-in-prompting)). **NovelAI's Lorebook** triggers on keywords or regex within a scan range of up to 10,000 characters ([NovelAI docs](https://docs.novelai.net/en/text/lorebook)). Both are deterministic and transparent, and neither does semantic retrieval over the writer's own earlier chapters or cites where a fact came from. Sudowrite users complain that rewrites change Story Bible names. Reports of context loss beyond 80K words are secondhand ([Sudowrite feedback](https://feedback.sudowrite.com/p/better-editing-rewriting-drafts-scenes-and-chapter-splitting-following-manuscript); [aitooltier](https://aitooltier.com/tools/sudowrite)). General assistants cap project files at **5–40 files** in ChatGPT Projects (third-party figures) ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/chatgpt-projects-file-limit/)). Claude Projects switch to RAG automatically near the context limit, "by up to 10x" capacity ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11473015-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-for-projects)), but a GitHub issue reports the switch firing far earlier than documented ([GitHub #25759](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/25759)). The local-first precedent is Obsidian: Copilot's Vault QA and Smart Connections run fully local embeddings with cited notes, but must re-index on every device ([obsidian-copilot](https://github.com/MartletH/obsidian-copilot); [insiderllm](https://insiderllm.com/guides/obsidian-local-llm-guide/)).

The stakes justify verification. In a 2025 JMIR study, GPT-4o fabricated **19.9%** of 176 citations, and **54.6%** of the real ones had bibliographic errors, most often wrong DOIs ([JMIR Mental Health](https://mental.jmir.org/2025/1/e80371)). The share of PubMed Central papers containing a fabricated reference rose from **1 in 2,828 (2023) to 1 in 277 (early 2026)**. That audit does not attribute every case to LLMs ([Badalova & Mayr, arXiv:2607.22693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22693)). Even domain RAG tools hallucinated on **17–34%** of legal queries in Stanford's older 2024 study ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-queries)).

## ByAuthorX has a strong provenance core but a basic retrieval engine

The audit of version 0.2.0 finds strong engineering wherever safety and provenance are involved:

- Document and chunk IDs are content hashes, so renames never orphan citations ([libraries.py:38-39, 155, 371](../../../src/byax/libraries.py)).
- Parsing runs in an isolated subprocess limited to 768 MiB and 30 s of CPU ([library_parser.py:52-69](../../../src/byax/library_parser.py)).
- Three per-provider consent categories exist, and `local_only` libraries can never go remote ([retrieval.py:102-143](../../../src/byax/retrieval.py)).
- Context is framed as "untrusted data, never instructions" ([providers.py:1312-1333](../../../src/byax/providers.py)).
- Review evidence must quote an exact substring of a supplied source and is labelled quotation, paraphrase or inference ([providers.py:1693-1738](../../../src/byax/providers.py)).

These are the properties competitors lack. They are also hard to retrofit, so every recommendation below preserves them.

Retrieval quality, by contrast, sits at a basic keyword baseline. The table maps each layer against the state of the art.

Several gaps are small bugs rather than missing capabilities: the unwired hybrid path, attachment-order concatenation, hard-coding `kind: 'paraphrase'` in the GUI cite picker ([gui_libraries.py:517](../../../src/byax/gui_libraries.py)), silent use of partial vector sets, and re-embedding chunks whose fingerprint has not changed. One effect of the current prompt layout is an inference, not a measurement. Because the volatile selection is serialized into the system instruction, any change to the selection rewrites the start of the prompt. That defeats provider prefix caching for the whole conversation.

## The state of the art: hybrid retrieval, enriched chunks and reranking

### Retrieval pipelines converge on three moves

The most-cited 2024–26 result is still Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval. It prepends a 50–100-token LLM-written situating context to each chunk before embedding and BM25 indexing. Top-20 failure rates fell from **5.7% to 3.7%** with contextual embeddings, to **2.9%** with contextual BM25 added, and to **1.9%** with reranking: a **67%** reduction. The one-time cost was about **$1.02 per million document tokens** with prompt caching ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). These are vendor figures. Gains vary by domain, from about 41% on code to about 84% on fiction, and critics question whether 67% is a best case or an average ([Almond AI critique](https://medium.com/almond-ai/responding-to-anthropics-contextual-retrieval-for-rag-apps-why-context-is-not-all-you-need-af503985aa55)). The fiction result matters for ByAuthorX's novelists.

Late chunking needs no LLM. It embeds the whole document, then pools per chunk, and adds roughly **+1.5–1.9 nDCG@10 points** on BEIR ([arXiv 2409.04701](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04701v1)). Semantic chunkers are inconsistent. A secondhand 2026 benchmark found they produced 43-token fragments and 54% accuracy, against 69% for recursive 512-token chunks ([premai summary](https://www.premai.io/blog/rag-chunking-strategies-the-2026-benchmark-guide/)). Context enrichment beats clever split points.

For local embeddings, **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B** gives the best quality per megabyte among permissively licensed models. It is Apache-2.0, 1024-dimensional with Matryoshka truncation down to 32 dimensions, has a 32K-token context, and scores **64.33** MTEB-multilingual and **70.70** English-v2. On the same card, BGE-M3 scores 59.56 and OpenAI text-embedding-3-large 58.93 multilingual ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). EmbeddingGemma-300M runs in under 200 MB quantized but is gated under the Gemma Terms of Use ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/embeddinggemma/)). Jina v3 and NV-Embed-v2 are non-commercial, and jina-embeddings-v4's license is contradictory across sources ([HF jina-v4](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-embeddings-v4/blob/c3adddde53298a4123684a1312f78ad3d56ae5d1/README.md)). Cloud options are gemini-embedding-001 (about $0.15/M tokens), OpenAI text-embedding-3 ($0.02–0.13/M) and Voyage-4. Voyage-4's models share one embedding space across sizes but have only vendor evaluations. All of these prices are secondhand ([premai](https://blog.premai.io/best-embedding-models-for-rag-2026-ranked-by-mteb-score-cost-and-self-hosting)).

Reranking is the largest late-stage gain. Published per-query latencies for the top 20 candidates are about **132 ms** (jina-reranker-v2), **241 ms** (bge-reranker-v2-m3) and **831 ms** (Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B), all on unstated hardware ([arXiv 2605.05726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05726); [arXiv 2605.06132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06132)). Reasoning-heavy queries are where keyword and embedding retrieval fail most. On BRIGHT, BM25 scores about 14.5 nDCG@10, while an LLM reranker lifts ReasonIR-8B to **36.9** ([BRIGHT](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7a0f8055c838df8e62329a76c7c6403d-Abstract-Conference.html); [ReasonIR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.20595)). Thematic writer questions ("where do my sources discuss X's motive?") look like BRIGHT, not like known-item search. Small ColBERT models offer a middle path: answerai-colbert-small-v1 (33M parameters) scores 53.79 on BEIR against ColBERTv2's 50.02 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/AnswerDotAI/answerai-colbert-small-v1)). It can serve as a CPU reranker without storing a multi-vector index.

For storage, **sqlite-vec** keeps vectors in the same SQLite file as FTS5. It is brute-force only, but binary quantization shrinks 1M vectors to about 32 MB, with oversampling and re-scoring recommended ([sqlite-vec guide](https://https-alexgarcia_xyz.proxy.bugsmash.io/sqlite-vec/guides/binary-quant.html)). An anecdotal figure puts a 500K × 768-dimensional scan at about 500 ms ([SitePoint](https://www.sitepoint.com/local-first-rag-vector-search-in-sqlite-with-hamming-distance/)). LanceDB is the embedded upgrade path if ANN indexes are needed ([DuckDB blog](https://duckdb.org/2026/05/21/test-driving-lance)).

For parsing, **Docling** (MIT code, permissive default weights) keeps page and bounding-box provenance and is competitive on CPU ([Docling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17887)). Marker v2 reports the best olmOCR-bench score (76.0), but that is self-reported and its Surya weights restrict companies above $5M revenue or funding. MinerU 4.x adds custom terms on top of Apache-2.0 ([Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/docling-vs-mineru-vs-marker-pdf-parser)).

### Deterministic search is far from exhausted

ByAuthorX uses only a fraction of what FTS5 offers without any AI. Available features include `porter` stemming, a `trigram` tokenizer for substring and mid-word matching, `remove_diacritics 2`, prefix indexes for search-as-you-type, per-column BM25 weights, NEAR, phrase and column filters, and `snippet()`/`highlight()`. External-content tables are kept in sync by triggers, and the `fts5vocab` term statistics support "did you mean" suggestions and more-like-this queries ([SQLite FTS5](https://sqlite.org/fts5.html)). RapidFuzz (MIT) adds typo tolerance over that vocabulary ([RapidFuzz](https://github.com/rapidfuzz/RapidFuzz)). pyahocorasick (BSD-3) can scan a manuscript for every codex name and alias in one pass ([pyahocorasick](https://pyahocorasick.readthedocs.io/)). That is how Novelcrafter-style mention tracking can be built, with case-sensitivity flags for names like "Will" and exclusion lists ([Novelcrafter](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/docs/codex/codex-tracking)). Obsidian's backlinks and unlinked-mentions pane and Scrivener's self-updating saved searches are the reference designs for project-wide context ([Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/backlinks); [L&L](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/use-scriveners-project-search-to-find-anything-in-your-projects)).

For linking a manuscript passage to a source passage that may change, the W3C Web Annotation selectors are the established model ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/)). Hypothesis stores three selectors per anchor: a range (structural), a text position, and a text quote with prefix and suffix. It re-anchors in that order, falling back to fuzzy matching with diff-match-patch's Bitap algorithm ([Hypothesis](https://web.hypothes.is/blog/fuzzy-anchoring/)). ByAuthorX already stores block IDs and offsets, so adding prefix and suffix context to the `quote` field would let citations survive source edits instead of going `stale`.

## Decide per request: routing beats both always-retrieve and always-long-context

Long context does not make retrieval obsolete for manuscripts. Chroma tested 18 models and found that all of them degrade as input grows. Degradation was faster when question and answer shared few words and when near-duplicate distractors were present. Focused prompts of about 300 tokens beat full ~113K-token prompts on LongMemEval for every model ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). NoLiMa found **11 of 13** long-context models below half their short-context accuracy at 32K tokens once literal word overlap was removed ([arXiv 2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)). A novel makes this worse than most corpora. "What colour were her eyes in chapter 3?" shares almost no words with the answer, and earlier drafts and similar scenes act as distractors. Anthropic's rule of thumb is to skip RAG below about 200K tokens and send everything with prompt caching ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). That rule fits holistic tasks like pacing critique, not precision lookups.

The research line from Self-RAG and FLARE through Corrective RAG, Adaptive-RAG and Self-Route points to one design. A cheap classifier picks the retrieval depth, and a sufficiency check escalates one tier when the evidence is weak ([Adaptive-RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14403); [CRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884); [Self-Route](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833)). These papers are cited from abstracts and were not re-read for this report. Self-Route stayed close to long-context accuracy at much lower cost. Coding agents moved from vector RAG to agentic grep and glob. Anthropic's Claude Code team said agentic search "worked better", but the evidence is talks and social posts and is specific to precise, lexical code ([AI Engineer talk](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/UM6sFg_jdlE-rag-is-dead-right-kuba-rogut)). Prose needs lexical, semantic and entity-indexed retrieval together.

ByAuthorX should adopt five explicit routes, chosen by rules first and a small classifier later, and always shown to the user:

| Route | Typical requests | Context assembled | Escalates to |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 selection-only | "Tighten this", grammar, tone | Selection, style profile, protections | — |
| R1 local | "Continue this scene" | Selection, ±N blocks, scene/chapter summary, codex entries detected by Aho-Corasick | R2 if an unknown entity is referenced |
| R2 targeted lookup | "What did I name the innkeeper?", "find a source for this claim" | Codex/glossary first, then hybrid FTS5 + vectors across the manuscript and libraries, reranked; cited spans | R3 if a CRAG-style grader says the evidence is insufficient |
| R3 agentic multi-hop | "Every place Mara lies to Tom", literature synthesis | Tool loop over `search_text`, `semantic_search`, `get_outline`, `read_range`, `get_entity`, with a call budget | R4 or abstain |
| R4 full-document | "Assess act 2 pacing", whole-book summary | Cached manuscript snapshot or hierarchical chapter summaries | — |

Routing signals are cheap and deterministic: presence of a selection, imperative versus interrogative verbs, codex hits, and scope words ("whole book", "chapter 4"). Privacy outranks every other routing rule. ByAuthorX already enforces `local_only` in the context assembler rather than the model picker ([retrieval.py:136-143](../../../src/byax/retrieval.py)), which is the right layer. Learned routers such as RouteLLM cut cost by "over 2 times in certain cases" at equal quality ([arXiv 2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)), but for a BYOK app a visible, policy-first rule set ("Local: document marked private") earns more trust than a black box.

Cache economics make R4 affordable if the prompt is laid out correctly. Current Anthropic cache reads cost **0.1x** base input (**0.05x** on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5), with a 512-token minimum, a 5-minute default TTL and up to four breakpoints. Toggling citations invalidates the system and message caches ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). OpenAI reads cost 0.1x on GPT-5.6 and later, and reuse "requires the entire rendered prefix to match" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). Gemini's implicit cache discount is 75% ([Google](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-2-5-models-now-support-implicit-caching/)). An illustrative 150K-token manuscript at $3/MTok costs about $0.45 per turn uncached and about $0.02–0.05 cached. Getting there means ordering the prompt as tools, then system, then project memory, then the manuscript snapshot (refreshed only at checkpoints), then conversation, then volatile items last. ByAuthorX currently does the opposite by serializing selection-bearing context into the system instruction. The Manus team calls KV-cache hit rate "the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent" ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)). Local llama.cpp, vLLM and Ollama prefix reuse follows the same ordering rule.

Memory should be explicit and editable. The 2025–26 production pattern is project-scoped memory that users can view, edit and delete ([Anthropic memory](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory)). Anthropic reports **+39%** on an internal agentic-search eval from its memory tool plus context editing, and **84%** fewer tokens from context editing in a 100-turn task. Both are internal, unreplicated figures ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/context-management)). For ByAuthorX, memory means versioned project artifacts: style profile, codex/character bible, glossary and timeline, all stored in the .byax model and edited through its undo stack. The assistant would add facts only as proposals ("Remember: Mara's eyes are grey, ch. 3 ¶12?") that carry provenance, and those flow through the existing review-proposal machinery.

MCP is where ByAuthorX can leapfrog. The 2026-07-28 revision made the protocol stateless and promoted MCP Apps and Tasks to official extensions ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/tags/mcp/)). The 2025-11-25 revision returns validation errors as tool errors so agents can self-correct ([MCP changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/changelog)). ByAuthorX already exposes bounded `context`, `search` and `read` ([mcp.py:50-76](../../../src/byax/mcp.py); [transport_policy.py:36-46](../../../src/byax/transport_policy.py)). The next step is a small, non-overlapping read tool set that includes `hybrid` search, an entity lookup and an outline. External agents and the in-app R3 loop would share the same verified retrieval. Prompt injection remains unsolved, so the "lethal trifecta" rule applies: never combine private data, untrusted content and an egress tool without confirmation ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). Spotlighting retrieved chunks with provenance delimiters cut attack success from above 50% to below 2% on older GPT models ([arXiv 2403.14720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720)). That is a mitigation, not a guarantee, and ByAuthorX's current framing is a good start.

## Grounding should be a cascade, cheapest check first

Native citation APIs differ in ways that matter. **Anthropic's Citations API** returns the verbatim `cited_text` with source-side locations: character ranges for text, pages for PDFs, and block ranges for custom content. Pointers are "guaranteed" valid, `cited_text` costs no output tokens, and custom-content documents keep caller-supplied block boundaries. ByAuthorX chunk IDs can therefore map one-to-one onto citable blocks. It is, however, incompatible with structured outputs ([Anthropic Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). That constraint hits WORK mode's JSON `{reply, proposed_edits}` contract directly. **Gemini** returns answer-side segments mapped to grounding chunks, with optional confidence scores ([Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/grounding/search-suggestions?hl=hi)). Whether its offsets count bytes or characters was not confirmed. **Cohere** returns answer-side character spans with sources ([Cohere](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/rag-citations)). **OpenAI** file_search annotations reportedly point only at a file, not a chunk span ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/file-search-annotations-reference-a-file-rather-than-a-chunk/1367438)). No vendor publishes citation precision or recall figures.

ByAuthorX should normalize all of these, plus its own `[[cite:ID]]` protocol for local and OpenAI-compatible models, into one record: answer span → source chunk → source span → verbatim quote. Where a vendor gives no source span, the quote can be recovered by fuzzy-aligning the answer segment against the cited chunk. None of these APIs proves entailment, and Anthropic's guarantee only means that the quote exists.

Verification should run as a cascade, cheapest first:

1. **Deterministic checks.** Normalize Unicode (NFC), whitespace, curly versus straight quotes, soft hyphens and line-break hyphenation, and ellipses. Then try exact match, then fuzzy alignment with RapidFuzz or diff-match-patch Bitap. This step alone fixes the brittle exact-substring checks in [citations.py:342](../../../src/byax/citations.py), [library_checks.py:119](../../../src/byax/library_checks.py) and [checks.py:1056-1063](../../../src/byax/checks.py). Those checks will otherwise reject many genuine quotations from PDFs read by pypdf.
2. **Claim decomposition and local entailment.** Split AI-written sentences into claims and score each one against its cited span with a small local model. **HHEM-2.1-Open** has open weights and scores **71.8%** balanced accuracy on LLM-AggreFact. **Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B** scores **77.4%** but is **CC BY-NC 4.0**, so it is unusable commercially without a license ([Bespoke Labs](https://bespokelabs.ai/bespoke-minicheck); [Vectara](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)). Both figures are vendor-reported. "Verify with Caution" warns that even top factuality metrics mislead when treated as ground truth ([arXiv 2501.14883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14883)), and ALCE's NLI scoring cannot detect partial support ([ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627v2)).
3. **Optional LLM judge.** Use a different provider than the generator, only for low-confidence cases, and only under the existing consent rules.

Results should appear as categorical states, never as an uncalibrated percentage. ByAuthorX's five review outcomes (supported, contradicted, not_established, conflicting_sources, needs_interpretation) already fit Scite's supporting/contrasting/mentioning model ([Scite help](https://help.scite.ai/zh/article/what-is-scite-1gfjuck)). The cascade would turn "unmeasurable" paraphrase and inference into measured-but-advisory states.

Bibliography integrity is a separate check. The app should never let a model write a reference from memory; models only select among resolved library entries. Retraction Watch data is open through Crossref's `update-to` and `updated-by` relations, and as a daily CSV for offline lookup ([Crossref](https://crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/retraction-watch/)). Existing hallucinated-reference checkers catch up to **32 of 33** bad references but wrongly flag up to **53 of 71** real ones ([arXiv 2607.22693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22693)). A DOI or title search that finds nothing should therefore display as "unverified, please check", never "fake". The unused `citation_lookup` consent category is already the right gate for these lookups. Link rot makes archived copies worthwhile: **38%** of 2013 web pages were gone by 2023 ([Pew](https://pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2024/05/pl_2024.05.17_link-rot_report.pdf)). Zotero 7's read-only local API at `localhost:23119` needs no key ([Zotero docs](https://www.zotero.org/support/dev/web_api/v3/local_api)). Hayagriva (MIT/Apache-2.0) supports all 2,600+ official CSL styles, whose files are CC BY-SA 3.0 ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/hayagriva/0.8.1)). It is a Rust crate, and no Python binding was evaluated. Finally, an internal per-span provenance log (human-typed, AI-generated, AI-edited, pasted, plus model and sources) can generate APA, MLA and Chicago AI-use disclosures. Secondary sources disagree on APA's current AI-citation form ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/how-to-cite-chatgpt-and-ai-tools-apa-mla-chicago)). C2PA text manifests exist in spec v2.4, but implementations are uncertified and are stripped by ordinary copy and paste ([c2patxt](https://pypi.org/project/c2patxt/)), so treat them as an optional export.

## A three-horizon roadmap that preserves the local-first contract

The ordering follows one principle: build the measurement harness first, fix bugs that leave specified capabilities unused, then add models, and keep every network or model download behind the existing consent and `local_only` machinery.

### Medium term (one to two quarters)

The first priority is a **local semantic tier that needs no Ollama**. That means Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B through ONNX or GGUF with Matryoshka truncation to 256–512 dimensions. Download it only on explicit user action, verify it by hash, and keep it out of the base install, which matches the deferred "verified model download" in the guide ([library-and-citations-guide.md:104-105](../../library-and-citations-guide.md)). Pair it with **sqlite-vec** using binary vectors and float re-scoring, behind an interface that can later swap in LanceDB. Keep FTS available immediately and fill in vectors asynchronously.

Second, add a **cross-encoder reranker** (bge-reranker-v2-m3, int8 ONNX) over the fused top 30–50, with an "accurate" toggle. Third, replace the chunker with **structure-aware chunking**: merge short paragraphs up to about 300–500 tokens, prepend the heading path, and keep parent-section pointers for small-to-big expansion. Fourth, offer **contextual retrieval** as an opt-in background job, run by a local model or, with consent, a cloud model with caching. Late chunking is the zero-LLM alternative.

Fifth, ship the **R0–R4 router with a "What the AI saw" inspector**. It would show the route and the reason, each context item with its token count and pin/exclude controls, the model and whether it is local or cloud, cache hits, and estimated cost. Add CRAG-style escalation and an explicit "not found in your manuscript or library" abstention. Sixth, add **manuscript-as-corpus**: index the open .byax document and sibling project documents at block granularity using the existing stable block IDs. Add Aho-Corasick codex/alias detection, backlinks and unlinked mentions, project-wide find, and saved searches.

Seventh, start the **verification cascade**: claim splitting plus HHEM-2.1-Open scoring of each chat or WORK claim against its cited chunk, shown as supported, partial or unsupported underlines. Eighth, add an **optional Docling quality path** for PDFs with poor text layers, page and bbox provenance, and annotation import. Ninth, activate **`citation_lookup`**: Crossref/DataCite DOI resolution with fuzzy title, author and year comparison, OpenAlex fallback, a retraction check against the Crossref relations or an offline CSV, and the Zotero 7 local API for reads.

### Ambitious differentiators (12 months)

ByAuthorX can own claim-level grounding in a word processor. Every AI-originated sentence would carry a provenance record, a source span, a W3C-style three-selector anchor that re-anchors fuzzily when either side is edited, and an entailment state. Citations would render as real CSL footnotes and bibliography entries. They would be version-pinned by content hash, so the "stale draft" failure Office Watch observed in Word is detected rather than hidden.

The agentic R3 search loop should be exposed as MCP tools, so in-app and external agents share one verified retrieval layer. It would run under the 2026-07-28 Tasks extension for long jobs and use plan-then-execute and dual-LLM patterns when untrusted imported content is in context ([arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08837v1)). Add a temporal story knowledge base, in which facts carry "valid as of chapter X" in the manner of Zep/Graphiti ([arXiv 2501.13956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956)), to power continuity checks. Offer RAPTOR-lite hierarchical chapter summaries for R4 on small local models. Add a bibliography health panel covering unresolved DOIs, metadata mismatches, retractions and dead links with archived copies. Generate AI-disclosure statements from the provenance log. Thematic reasoning queries are where LLM reranking roughly doubles nDCG on BRIGHT, so offer an LLM reranker in R3, as a BYOK or local option, for those queries only.

## Measure retrieval and grounding separately, per provider

The evaluation plan follows the published consensus: score retrieval apart from generation, use small hand-labelled domain sets, and validate LLM judges against humans ([RAGAS](https://docs.ragas.io/en/v0.1.21/concepts/metrics/); [premai](https://blog.premai.io/rag-evaluation-metrics-frameworks-testing-2026)). Build three to five fixture libraries from redistributable material: a public-domain novel with deliberately varied earlier drafts as distractors, an arXiv PDF set with known references, a humanities set with footnote-style citations, and a web-clip set. Label 100 or more queries per library across five types: known-item quote, factual lookup, thematic or reasoning, citation-locating ("which page says…"), and manuscript continuity ("what did I name…").

Track recall@5, recall@20 (Anthropic's metric is 1 − recall@20), nDCG@10 and MRR for every configuration. The configurations to compare are keyword baseline, Q4 query hygiene, hybrid, hybrid plus rerank, and contextual chunks. Record p50/p95 latency and index size and build time against Nielsen's 0.1 s and 1 s thresholds ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). The targets suggested in the notes are under 300 ms for hybrid retrieval and under 1 s with reranking, to be measured on named reference laptops. The docs already refuse to claim numbers without reference hardware ([library-and-citations-guide.md:100](../../library-and-citations-guide.md)).

For generation, measure five things. Citation precision and recall follow ALCE: does each cited span support its sentence, and is each factual sentence cited? Add quote-verification pass rates before and after normalization, entailment-checker agreement with a human-labelled sample, abstention correctness on unanswerable questions, and route accuracy for the router. Report each per provider and per local model, because abstention behaviour differs across vendors ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). Use a judge model from a different vendor than the generator.

Gate releases on regressions in CI using recorded fixtures. Run live-provider suites on demand, which would also close issues #29 and #30, the unverified live semantic evidence and provider integrations ([issue-audit-2026-10-14.md:28-31](../../issue-audit-2026-10-14.md)). Keep traces local by default. Feed accept/reject decisions on proposals back into the golden set only with explicit user opt-in.

### What this evidence cannot yet tell you

Several load-bearing numbers are **vendor-reported** and unreplicated:

- Anthropic's 67% contextual-retrieval reduction and its memory and context-editing gains.
- Marker's olmOCR-bench lead.
- Qwen3 and EmbeddingGemma MTEB scores.
- MiniCheck and HHEM accuracies.
- Voyage-4 quality.

Many **competitor details are secondary-sourced**: Word reference limits, NotebookLM source caps, ChatGPT file caps, Notion agents, the Gemini Notebook rename, Microsoft Researcher's use of Claude, and the "Windsurf/Cline dropped vector indexes" claim. The date of Word deep citations is contested. Several **academic papers** (Self-RAG, FLARE, CRAG, Adaptive-RAG, Self-Route, RULER) were cited from abstracts without re-reading. Reranker latency figures lack hardware details.

No source benchmarks agentic lexical search against vector RAG and long context on manuscript or fiction tasks, and none measures CPU embedding throughput for 0.6B models on laptops. Both gaps are why the in-house harness comes first. Licenses for sqlite-vec, bge-reranker, answerai-colbert, Qwen3-Reranker, HHEM, MiniCheck small variants, citeproc-py and GROBID were not verified.

**Competitors not covered** include:

- Apple Pages with Apple Intelligence.
- Microsoft Recall and the Graph semantic index.
- Perplexity Pages/Spaces, ChatGPT Canvas and Gemini Gems.
- Scrivener, Ulysses, Dabble, Plottr and Atticus.
- Jenni AI, Paperpal, Writefull, Overleaf AI, SciSpace, Elicit (current features), Consensus (in depth) and Scite's 2026 API.
- Mem, Tana, Heptabase, Logseq, AnythingLLM, GPT4All LocalDocs and Khoj.
- Coda and Craft.

Also not researched: publisher AI-disclosure policies for 2026, user studies of citation-chip over-reliance, and feature-level changes to MCP resources, sampling and elicitation in the 2026-07-28 revision.

## Conclusion

Competition in grounded writing is no longer about who can attach the most files. Microsoft and Google both already let users pin large corpora. The open question is who can show, sentence by sentence, that a claim is supported by a specific, version-pinned passage, and who can explain what the model saw and why. That favours ByAuthorX's architecture over cloud suites, because content-hash identities, locator-precise marks, consent-gated payloads and evidence outcomes are prerequisites that competitors would have to retrofit. ByAuthorX's weakness, retrieval recall and precision, is the most commoditized layer in the stack: permissively licensed small models and SQLite extensions now cover it on a laptop.

The main risk is not technical but evidential. Without a golden set, every improvement in this roadmap is a guess, and the field's headline numbers are mostly vendor-run. ByAuthorX should build the harness first, publish its own retrieval and citation-accuracy benchmarks on reference hardware, and treat measured grounding as a product feature in its own right.
